# Motivation draft (Introduction section)

> Draft only — to be refined and integrated with citations during manuscript writing.

Foundation models have rapidly become the dominant paradigm in computational pathology, with general-purpose encoders such as UNI, CONCH, Prov-GigaPath, and Virchow now routinely used across a wide range of downstream tasks, from tissue classification to biomarker prediction. Several recent reviews have already surveyed this broader landscape of pathology foundation models. However, a more specific and rapidly growing sub-field remains unmapped: the use of foundation models to bridge histopathology whole-slide imaging with spatial transcriptomics (ST). In the past 18 months alone, at least eight to nine dedicated methods (e.g., OmiCLIP/Loki, STORM, DeepSpot-M, SpaFoundation, FmH2ST) have been published, yet no systematic review has synthesized this literature.

This gap is practically motivated. Spatial transcriptomics remains costly and low-throughput compared to routine histology, which is acquired for nearly every cancer patient. If foundation models can reliably infer spatially resolved gene expression from H&E images alone, this could substantially widen access to molecular characterization of the tumor microenvironment, particularly in settings where ST is not feasible at scale. Understanding which methods work, on which platforms and tissue types, and how comparable their reported results actually are, is therefore a question of direct relevance to the field, not only a bibliographic exercise.

Unlike prior surveys that catalog methods individually, we place explicit emphasis on comparability: differences in preprocessing (patch size, magnification, staining normalization), evaluation metrics, and validation cohorts often make reported performance difficult to compare across studies. By systematically extracting these methodological details alongside reported outcomes, we aim to assess not only what has been done, but whether the resulting body of evidence supports robust conclusions about relative model performance.

Finally, we connect this systematic mapping to a second, narrative strand of inquiry: interpretability. A recurring open question in the field is whether foundation model embeddings capture biologically meaningful signal beyond what classical, human-interpretable morphometric features (e.g., nuclear size, eccentricity, texture) already provide — and whether this added signal explains, at least in part, why FM-based approaches outperform classical deconvolution methods in ST-related tasks. Recent interpretability work (e.g., concept decomposition frameworks such as PICASSO, representational similarity analyses across models) offers early evidence on this question, which we integrate into our discussion.

## Note

- This review is scoped to methods that *explicitly* integrate histology and spatial transcriptomics (Section A). General-purpose pathology foundation models (UNI, CONCH, Prov-GigaPath, Virchow) are out of scope as primary inclusions unless a paper applies one of them, as-is, to an ST-related task — see `docs/backbone-models.md` for the reference table and rationale.
- Open question worth tracking during full-text extraction: how many included studies explicitly compare a general-purpose backbone (used as-is) against an ST-specific pretrained model? If enough studies make this comparison, it could become a dedicated sub-result rather than a methods-table footnote.
